How Measurement Can Impact Your Performance

Understanding Your Media Investment

Your digital marketing budget is the foundation of your campaign reach. This includes all spending on display advertising and Connected TV (CTV) campaigns.

The CPM (Cost Per Mille) represents how efficiently you're buying media. Lower CPMs mean more impressions for your budget, while higher CPMs often indicate premium placements or targeted audiences.

💡 Tip: A blended CPM of $15-25 is typical for financial services. Premium placements and highly targeted audiences may cost $30-50 CPM.

Why this matters:

  • Budget and CPM determine your total impression volume
  • More impressions = more opportunities to reach prospects
  • Efficient CPMs maximize your campaign reach

Measuring Campaign Engagement

💡 Benchmark: Financial services typically see 0.10-0.25% CTR for display ads. CTV campaigns often have lower direct CTR but higher brand lift.

Current site visitors provides context for your existing funnel performance. Understanding your baseline helps measure the true incremental impact of new campaigns.

What to expect:

  • Display ads: 0.10-0.20% CTR average
  • CTV campaigns: Focus on view-through attribution
  • Financial products: Lower CTR but higher intent

Optimizing Your Funnel

You know your metrics best. These are your numbers but we've provided some industry benchmark numbers for you to compare against. Your conversion funnel shows where prospects drop off between awareness and closing. Each stage represents a critical decision point.

Application Start Rate: The bridge between browsing and serious intent. Higher rates indicate strong value proposition and clear calls-to-action.

Completion Rate: Measures friction in your application process. Abandonment often happens due to complexity or time requirements.

Understanding Account Lifetime Value

Lifetime Value (LTV) represents the total profit you earn from each checking/savings account over its lifetime. Here's what the research shows:

💡 Industry Estimates: Average customer lifetime value ranges from $2,000 to $4,500, with significant variation by customer segment.

The 90/10 Rule: Retail banking follows a 90/10 business model where 10% of customers account for 90% of profits. This means CLV distribution is highly skewed:

  • Super-profitable customers: ~10% of customers provide 67% of total relationship value
  • Unprofitable customers: ~40% of customers provide just 1.4% of total value
  • High-value customers: Can reach $10,000+ CLV (young professionals with mortgage/business loan potential)
  • Low-value customers: Fee-sensitive, low-balance accounts may have negative lifetime value

Key Variables That Drive CLV:

  • Product Mix: Multi-product customers (checking + savings + loans + credit cards) have much higher CLV
  • Balance Levels: Higher balances generate more revenue through interest spreads
  • Fee Generation: Usage of fee-based services boosts profitability
  • Relationship Length: Banking relationships often last decades
  • Cross-Selling Success: Moving customers from basic checking to mortgages, investments, etc.

Present Value Considerations: When discounted to present value using typical bank hurdle rates (8-12%), nominal lifetime values are reduced. With average acquisition cost at $390, banks need CLV well above that threshold to be profitable.

Measurement Lift: Industry studies show measurement-driven optimization typically delivers 7-15% lift in campaign performance through better targeting, creative optimization, channel allocation, and waste reduction.
